1981 · Nintendo

Octopus: Graphics

Every shape is fixed on the glass. The diver moves by lighting the next shape along his path, and a tentacle grows by lighting one more segment.

01 · Everything that moves

Each object, through every place it can be

Each figure lights its object's shapes in the order the game does, one change a step at the start of Game A.

At the start of Game A a step takes about 0.21 s, and each tentacle moves once every four steps, so a segment about every 0.86 s. The diver moves as fast as the presses come, one place a press.

The diver's six places are six figures drawn whole; the bag he carries is a shape of its own at each of the five places in the water and in the boat. At the chest three more shapes are his arms: standing, reaching into the chest and lifting the treasure out.

02 · The bits behind the shapes

Where each object lives in memory

Each shape is one bit of one cell. Hover over or tap a row to light its shapes.

A cell's four bits drive the four shapes of one LCD latch; rows 0 and 1 of memory are the two halves of the screen ($0640). The diver's path and the tentacles cross from cell to cell, so the code that moves them names each bit it passes.

03 · Shapes that stay put

The boat, the alarm and the clock

The octopus's body is the digits' extra segment of the middle digit, like the clock's colon and the alarm's bolts: the chip's digit table has one spare segment for each of the three digits, and the game uses all three for pictures ($065B).
