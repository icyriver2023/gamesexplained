1981 · Nintendo

Octopus: Discoveries

What the program does that the manual, the reviews and the face of the unit do not say.

01 · Chance

The octopus throws no dice

The four tentacles move in a fixed turn, one segment a step, and their only choices are read from the clock's seconds ($0100). An empty tentacle waits while the seconds end in 8 or 9 and starts again on any other second. The forked tentacle reaches for the rope when the tens of seconds are even and toward the diver's second place when they are odd ($0180). In Game B one bit of the seconds decides whether a shrinking tentacle stops at its base. A player who watches the clock's seconds, which the unit keeps counting through the game, knows which way the forked tentacle will go before it moves.

02 · Speed

190 points is faster than 200

The wait before each step comes from the score's hundreds digit plus its tens digit ($00C0), so the game is at its fastest at 690 to 699, 780 to 799, 870 to 899 and 960 to 999, and slows down again every time the hundreds turn over: at 190 the wait is 7 passes, at 200 it is 10. Game B is faster than Game A only at 0 to 19 points and at 100 to 109; everywhere else the two run at the same speed. What keeps Game B harder is its tentacles' habit of growing back from the base.

The tentacles also speed up while the diver is taking treasure: the wait is two passes shorter until the take is over, just when he cannot move.

03 · A safe moment

The octopus stops while you are paid

On the step the treasure is lifted, and on each of the three steps the boat counts the delivery in, the step skips the tentacle's turn ($0375, $0367). Each return to the boat freezes the octopus for three steps, and each piece of treasure for one.

04 · The bonus

No bonus for a perfect game

At 200 and 500 points the game gives the lost divers back, but only when one is missing: with both spares in the boat there is no blinking and no pause ($0085, the test at $009B). At 999 the next point makes 000, with no bonus, and the speed goes back to the start's.

05 · Things the review got wrong

Both buttons do nothing, and the diver cannot go home empty-handed

A buyer's review posted to Usenet in 1982 said that pressing both buttons makes the diver dance back and forth to dodge the tentacles. The program reads left and right together as neither, and the diver stays where he is ($0380). The same review describes the diver climbing back into the boat; he can do that only with treasure. On the rope without any, a press of left leaves him on the rope ($0340).

A diver left in the boat goes back down the rope by himself after 8 seconds, unless the forked tentacle is reaching up toward the rope at that moment ($03EB).

06 · The test pads

The invincibility pad also repeats the buttons

MAME's notes give the board an unpopulated "Invincibility" pad on the chip's R2 row. The program skips the catch while any key of that row is held ($01C0), and it also lets a held left or right repeat four times a second, which normally only happens when the time is being set ($0380). With it held, holding right takes the diver to the chest without a single extra press.

MAME marks the R3 row "alarm test?". Its fourth key rings the alarm at once; its third puts the unit back into the state it powers up in, every segment lit and the time to be set, with the seconds at 00 ($059E).

07 · Buttons

Three keys at once open the alarm

The program works out the mode from the keys held by adding to them and testing the carries ($0540). One key gives its own mode, but Time, Game A and Game B held together add up to the alarm's setting.
